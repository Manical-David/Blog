我真的很累。

每天早上五点半起床，晚上十一点还在写作业。我已经很久没有在十二点之前睡过觉了。有时候写着写着，趴在桌子上就睡着了，醒来发现作业本上沾满了口水，又得重新写。

爸妈总说你要努力，你要考上好高中，好大学，以后才能有出路。可是没有人问我累不累，没有人问我快不快乐。

班主任每天都在耳边念分数念排名。月考完了念期中，期中完了念期末，期末完了还要念模拟考。我的名字在班级二十多名，不算差，但也不算好。每次开会，班主任只看前三名和后三名，中间的学生好像不存在一样。

我讨厌考试。讨厌那种坐在教室里，手心出汗，心跳加速的感觉。讨厌考完试之后对答案的声音，讨厌看排名时那种被人盯着看的眼神。

爸妈会问我考了多少分，排了多少名。然后叹气，然后沉默。那种沉默比骂我还难受。

我不知道读书到底是为了什么。为了考试？为了分数？为了让他们有面子？

老师说要感恩，说父母不容易。可是谁来心疼心疼我？我才十四岁，我的肩膀还没有长好，为什么要让 我扛这么多东西？

同学之间也不轻松。没有人会停下来等你，大家都往前冲。考好了有人酸，考差了有人笑。没有人真的关心你过得好不好，只关心你考了多少分。

我真的受够了。

有时候我想，如果我消失几天，会不会有人发现？会不会有人着急？可是想想，肯定只会换来一顿骂——你怎么这么不懂事？你怎么这么不争气？

这个世界好奇怪。大人总是说他们小时候有多苦有多累，可是他们为什么还要让我们重复他们的路？他们明明知道不快乐，为什么还要把这种不快乐传给我们？

我不知道该跟谁说。跟爸妈说？他们只会说我不够努力。跟老师说？他们只会让我调整心态。跟同学说？大家都有自己的烦恼，谁顾得上谁。

我就这样一天一天熬着。不知道这样的日子什么时候是个头。

也许有一天，我会撑不住吧。

也许有一天，我也会变成他们口中那个“想不开”的孩子。

</p>

A tiredness has settled into my bones.

I wake before dawn at 5:30 and I'm still hunched over homework at eleven each night. I can't recall the last time I slept before midnight. Sometimes I fall asleep mid-sentence, my face resting on the desk; I wake to find my exercise book stained with drool and must start all over again.

My parents keep saying, “Work hard, get into a good high school, then a good university — that's the only chance you have.” But no one asks if I'm spent, if I'm permitted to be happy.

My homeroom teacher recites scores and rankings like scripture. After one test it's the midterms, after midterms it's the finals, after finals it's another round of mock exams. I hover around twentieth in class — not terrible, not remarkable. At meetings the teacher only looks at the top three and the bottom three; everyone in between is treated as if invisible.

I dread exams. The classroom makes my palms sweat and my heart race. I loathe the noisy chorus of answers after a test and the way people stare when ranks are announced.

My parents ask for the score, for the rank. Then they sigh. Then silence. That silence cuts deeper than any scolding.

I no longer know what we're studying for. For tests? For numbers on a page? For their pride?

Teachers tell us to be grateful, to remember how hard our parents had it. But who asks after how I feel? I'm only fourteen; my shoulders aren't broad enough yet — why must I carry so much?

My classmates offer no respite. No one pauses for someone who lags behind; everyone rushes forward. If you do well, someone resents you; if you do poorly, someone laughs. No one truly cares whether you're alright — only what your score is.

I'm bone-weary.

Sometimes I wonder: if I vanished for a few days, would anyone notice? Would anyone worry? I imagine the answer — a scolding: “How could you be so selfish? Why can't you try harder?”

This world feels strange. Adults speak of how hard their childhoods were, yet they insist we walk the same path. They knew it brought little joy — so why hand that emptiness down to us?

I don't know who to tell. Tell my parents? They'll say I'm not working hard enough. Tell my teachers? They'll tell me to adjust my mindset. Tell my friends? Everyone has their own burdens; who has room to carry someone else's?

So I trudge through each day. I don't know when this will end.

Maybe one day I won't be able to hold on.

Maybe one day I'll become the child they whisper about — the one who couldn't endure.
